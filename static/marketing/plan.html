The plan

How to get Shimmer in front of people

A plan for one person with a day job and no budget. It asks for about two hours a week. Everything in it is something you can do alone, tonight, for free.

1The short version

If you only read one paragraph, read this one.

People making AI music can hear that something is wrong with their tracks, but they cannot name it and they do not know it can be fixed. Your job is to name it for them. Post about the fizz, not about the app. Show the Removed feed, because hearing the noise on its own is the moment people understand. Do that once a week in the places those people already gather, answer every comment, and let the free price and the local install close the deal.

You are not running a campaign. You are becoming the person who explains AI audio artifacts. The app follows from that.

2What you are actually offering

Not a mastering tool. There are hundreds of those. Shimmer is the only one that treats AI generation noise as its own problem and fixes it before mastering, then proves the fix out loud.

Three things make it hard to copy:

  • It plays back what it removed. Nobody else hands you the proof and invites you to check.
  • It runs on your machine and costs nothing. Most rivals need an upload, an account, and a card.
  • It knows the order. Clean first, master second, and it stops you from mastering the same track twice.

Everything else, the EQ, the stems, the batch runs, is depth for people who stay. Lead with the fizz.

3Who you are talking to

GroupWhat they wantWhere they areHow much to chase
AI music makers Their track to stop sounding thin and fizzy, without learning audio engineering. Suno and Udio subreddits and Discord servers, AI music groups, YouTube comment sections. Most of your effort. This is the group.
Producers using AI parts A clean stem or vocal they can drop into a real session without fighting it. Production subreddits, plugin forums, mixing Discords. Some. They arrive skeptical and become your loudest fans.
Privacy and open source people Software that does not phone home, does not charge monthly, and can be read. Hacker News, self hosting and open source communities, Linux spaces. A little. One good post here brings a wave, then goes quiet.

4What people use now

Know what you are next to, and be fair about it. Never name a rival to insult it. Describe what Shimmer does and let people work out the rest.

What they useWhy they use itWhere Shimmer differs
Online mastering services Upload a file, get a louder file back, no learning needed. They master the noise along with the music. Shimmer removes it first, and never sees your file.
Repair and mastering plugins Powerful, trusted, and they do far more than Shimmer does. They cost real money and expect you to know what to reach for. Shimmer is free and picks for you.
Nothing at all Most people upload the raw generated file. This is your real competition. They do not know the noise has a name. Teach that first.

5The one idea to plant

Say this until you are sick of it

That fizz is not in your song. The model added it. It can be taken out, and you can hear it go.

Everything you post should push that idea forward. If a post does not, it can still be fun, but it is not doing the job.

7The first 30 days

Week 1 — get the shop window right

  • Read the top of the README as if you had never seen it. Fix anything that takes more than ten seconds to understand.
  • Record one 40 second clip: a track playing, then the Removed feed alone. No talking, no music bed. Just the noise.
  • Take one clean screenshot of the release check card saying the file is ready.
  • Join two Discord servers and three subreddits. Post nothing. Read the rules and answer two questions that have nothing to do with Shimmer.

Week 2 — say the thing

  • Post number 2, Name the problem, on the biggest AI music subreddit. No link in the post. Put it in a comment if people ask.
  • Reply to every comment for two days. Ask what their track sounds like.
  • Post the 40 second clip to YouTube with post 4 as the description.

Week 3 — introduce yourself

  • Post number 10, Who made it and why, in the community that treated you best in week 2.
  • Ask for one thing: tell me what it gets wrong on your track.
  • Fix the first bug someone reports, then say publicly that you fixed it. This is worth more than any post.

Week 4 — go where the doubters are

  • Post number 7, Loudness without squashing, somewhere producers read. Expect hard questions and answer them straight.
  • Write down every question you could not answer well. Those are next month's posts.
  • Count what happened. See section 10.

8The rhythm after that

Once a month, in this order. It should take one evening.

  1. Ship something. A fix, a preset, a small feature. There must be a reason to talk.
  2. Write what changed in plain words, the way the changelog does.
  3. Post it in the one or two places that answered last time.
  4. Answer everything for two days.
  5. Release a track as The Treq when you have one, and say what cleaned it.

Do not post on a schedule when you have nothing to say. A quiet month with a real fix beats four posts about nothing.

9What to build

Six things. Build them once, use them for a year.

  • The noise clip. 40 seconds. Track, then the Removed feed alone. Your single most convincing asset.
  • A before and after pair. Same track, levels matched, so people judge the sound and not the volume. Say that you matched them.
  • The verdict screenshot. The release check card saying the file is ready to upload.
  • A five minute walkthrough. Install, drop a file, Analyze, Clean and Master, download. Real time, including the wait.
  • A first run answer. A short note about the Windows download warning and what to click, so it never becomes a bad first impression.
  • Three preset examples. A fizzy track, a glassy vocal, a harsh mix, each with the preset Analyze chose and why.

10How to tell if it is working

You cannot count users, and that is on purpose. Shimmer never phones home, so there are no install numbers, no usage reports, and no way to see who is running it. That is a promise worth more than the data. Measure what people do in public instead.

What to countWhereA good first month
Release downloadsThe GitHub releases pageAny number above zero. Watch the direction, not the size.
StarsGitHubRough interest over time. Do not chase it.
Issues and bug reportsGitHubThree. Reports mean real people ran it on real tracks.
Comments that ask a questionReddit, DiscordMore than the upvotes. A question means someone is deciding.
Video watch timeYouTubeDid they stay for the Removed feed? That is the whole test.
People saying it out loudAnywhereOne person recommending Shimmer to someone else beats a hundred views.

Every month, write down the single best thing that happened and the single worst. Do more of the first. That is the whole review.

11What could go wrong

RiskWhat to do about it
A community treats you as a spammer Help for two weeks before you post anything of your own. Follow the self promotion rules to the letter. If a post is removed, ask the moderators what would be allowed, and thank them.
People argue about AI music itself Do not take the bait. Shimmer fixes audio problems. Post where AI music is welcome and let the rest go.
The Windows download warning scares people off Say it before they hit it. Explain what to click and that the launcher is short enough to read in a text editor.
Bug reports pile up faster than you can fix them Answer every one within a few days, even if the answer is not yet. Being reachable is the point.
Someone tests a claim and it does not hold This is the expensive one. Keep to the checked numbers in the message reference, and never say a thing you have not seen the app do.
You get bored The most likely ending. Two hours a week beats a burst and a year of silence. Cut the plan down before you drop it.

12More post ideas

When the ten are used up, these are the next angles. All true, all ready to write.

  • Split the song, keep the parts. Vocals, drums, bass and more, mixed on faders, with a lane holding whatever the split dropped.
  • See what happens to your audio. The Signal Chain tab draws every stage in order, with nothing hidden.
  • Hear an edit in about a second. Live preview loops a short part and re-renders as you change settings.
  • The fixed whistles nobody talks about. Thin steady tones high up that never move, found once and notched out.
  • Tags that stay with the file. Title, artist and the rest, written the way each format expects.
  • An EQ that suggests its own moves. Fix first, shape second, cuts before boosts, and nothing big.
  • Why the same settings always give the same result. No machine learning in the cleanup path.
  • What the strength slider really does, and how to find the gentlest setting that works.
