Reference library

Play music through this machine and Shimmer measures its tone, then throws the audio away and keeps only the curve. Developer tooling — not part of the app users see.

How to use it

  1. Start Shimmer the usual way (start.bat) and open this page at http://localhost:7860/static/references/. It will not work from a file:// URL — it talks to the running app.
  2. Turn off volume normalisation in your player. Spotify: Settings → Playback → Audio normalisation.
  3. Pick the output device you actually hear sound from.
  4. Press Start, then play music.
  5. Press Stop when you are done. That is all.

It follows the track for you. Windows reports what is playing, so each song is saved under its own name and the next one starts by itself when the title changes. Put a playlist on and walk away — you get one row per track, correctly named, without touching anything. Anything shorter than 20 seconds of music is dropped rather than saved as a bad average.

Aim for commercial releases by other people — that is the material the tone target is missing. The name box is only a fallback for players that do not report to Windows.

Turn off your player's volume normalisation first. Level itself does not matter — the curve is normalised, so a track played 12 dB louder measures identically. But a normaliser also limits when it raises a quiet track, and that changes the audio. Loudness is never recorded from a capture for the same reason. Needs about 20 s of music per track.
