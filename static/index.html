    Quick start
    
                
    
                    What is shimmer? It is the thin, fizzy, metallic noise
                    that AI music tools can leave in the high end of a track. You
                    hear it as sizzle on cymbals, a glassy sheen on vocals, or a
                    busy hiss up top. The model added it; it is not part of your
                    music. Shimmer helps you find that noise and turn it down, then
                    masters the song for release.
                
    

                
    Your first master, step by step
    
                
      
                    
    1. 1
                        Drop your track in. WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG and M4A all
                        work. MP3 and M4A need a free helper called ffmpeg (see
                        Setup).
      2. 
                    
    2. 2
                        Click Analyze (in the Analysis card, or step 2 in the
                        right column). Shimmer measures your song: its loudness, its
                        tone, and any fixed tones (steady whistles at one pitch). It
                        marks what it finds on the What do you hear? cards and
                        turns on Fixed tones when it finds steady tones. The
                        Analysis card then lists what it found as a checklist: tick
                        what you want and press Apply. Each row says whether
                        it is On now. It also
                        moves the preview loop to where the top end is busiest, and
                        plans a Suggested EQ: a ringing tone, mud or harshness
                        first, then a few gentle balance moves. You can apply those
                        moves in the Parametric EQ section, and every move stays
                        editable. Analyze does not change your audio.
      3. 
                    
    3. 3
                        Pick what you hear. Turn on each card under What do
                        you hear? that matches a problem you can hear. Each card
                        turns on one fix, such as a de-esser or a notch filter. The fix
                        shows under Fixes on, with an Amount slider. The
                        What do you hear? tab in this Help explains every
                        card.
      4. 
                    
    4. 4
                        Listen. Turn on Live in the Preview loop at the
                        bottom, and a short section loops. Press 2 to hear the
                        processed version, 1 to hear your original, and
                        3 to hear the Removed track. Switch back and forth
                        — that is the only way to really judge it. The preview
                        runs the same sound path as the export, level included, so the
                        loop sounds like the file you will get.
      5. 
                    
    5. 5
                        Adjust if you need to. Move a card's Amount and
                        the loop renders again. In Mastering, pick a
                        Loudness target: Commercial (the default),
                        Balanced or Streaming standard. Each choice shows
                        its LUFS level. Tone match and Tilt shape the
                        tone, or you can match a Reference track instead. The
                        EQ presets menu in the Parametric EQ holds starting
                        points, each one saying what it does.
      6. 
                    
    6. 6
                        Click Clean & Master (it says Clean when
                        mastering is off). The processing window ends on a
                        Download step; you can also download from the green
                        banner. In Settings you can have the file download by
                        itself, or land straight in a folder of yours with a Show in
                        folder button. The What changed chart under the
                        player shows the spectrum before and after, and what was
                        removed. The Release check card then measures the file
                        itself (loudness, true peak, clipping, start and end, tags and
                        more) and gives one verdict first: is it ready to upload? The
                        file carries its tags: title, artist and album from the
                        Tags section, plus a Shimmer note in the comment.
      7. 
                
    

                
    Remix: split the song into stems
    
                
      
                    
    1. 1
                        Drop the track on the Remix tab. Recent sessions with
                        finished stems say so and reload in a second.
      2. 
                    
    2. 2
                        Pick how many stems. The mixer asks first: Fast
                        (4 stems, about 10 seconds on a GPU), Best (4 stems from
                        the fine-tuned model, about 25 seconds), 6 stems (adds
                        guitar and piano), or the slower Ultra and Studio
                        blends (see Setup). Clicking a card separates; the window shows
                        the engine's real progress. Stems are kept per track and split,
                        so a card marked "cached" loads instantly, and the dock's
                        Separate button re-runs at another quality any time.
      3. 
                    
    3. 3
                        Mix in the lanes. One lane per stem with its own
                        waveform, mute, solo, fader and an FX rack (formant,
                        saturation, doubler, reverb), plus a Residual lane that
                        holds whatever the separator dropped, so the untouched mix is
                        your original. Every move re-renders the loop; press 1
                        for the original and 2 for the remix. The Quick
                        buttons make an instrumental, an acapella or a vocal lift.
      4. 
                    
    4. 4
                        Render & Download sums the lanes, runs the cleanup
                        picked under Artifact cleanup on export, masters and
                        writes the file. Auto cuts the steady tones it
                        finds; a preset turns on its matching card (see the What do
                        you hear? tab). Stems in the right column downloads
                        the parts themselves as a ZIP of 24-bit WAVs, as separated or
                        through your mix.
      5. 
                
    

                
    
                    The one habit worth building: press 3 and listen to
                    the Removed track. It plays exactly what the fixes took out,
                    and nothing else: tone match, EQ and mastering are not in it. The
                    player turns it up so you can hear it; that boost is never in your
                    file. It should sound like hiss, fizz, whistles or sizzle. If you
                    hear vocals, snare hits or melody in it, a fix is cutting into your
                    music. Lower that card's Amount until the music is gone from
                    Removed.
                
    

                
    Good to know
    
                
      
                    
    • Volume fools your ears. Louder almost always sounds
                    better, even when it is not. Loudness-matched A/B is on by
                    default so you compare the sound, not the level.
      • 
                    
    • Each fix works in its own band. The fixes for fizz,
                        sibilance, clicks and harshness work in the upper mids and
                        highs, well above the bass. Low-mid build-up works on the
                        low mids. With mastering on, a low-cut (high-pass filter)
                        removes only the deep rumble below the bass.
      • 
                    
    • The bar at the bottom is your transport. Skip 5
                        seconds either way, click or drag the scrubber to move, and
                        press Space to play. The amber band on the scrubber is
                        the Live loop.
      • 
                    
    • Nothing is permanent until you export. Cards, sliders
                    and EQ only change what you hear in the preview. Your original file
                    is never changed.
      • 
                
    

                
    
                    Tip: click the ? next to
                    a card or control for help on that one.
                
    
            
    

            
    
                
    What do you hear?
    
                
    
                    In 2.0 the Master tab has no preset list. You pick the cards
                    under What do you hear? that match what you hear, and
                    each card turns on one fix. Not sure? Answer a few questions
                    below, or click Analyze and let Shimmer mark what it can
                    measure.
                
    

                
    How the cards work
    
                
      
                    
    • Pick any. Turn on a card for each problem you hear.
                    Click a card again to turn it off.
      • 
                    
    • Found. After Analyze, a card it measured shows
                    Found and what it found, such as the pitch of a steady
                    tone. Analyze measures fixed tones and loudness. The other cards
                    are up to your ears.
      • 
                    
    • Fixes on. Each card you turn on adds a row under
                    Fixes on, with its fix and an Amount slider. Amount
                    sets how deep the fix may cut: 0% changes nothing, and 100% is the
                    deepest cut that fix allows. Most cards start halfway. Fixed tones
                    starts at full depth.
      • 
                    
    • Removed track. Press 3 to hear the Removed track.
                    It plays exactly what the fixes took out, turned up so you can hear
                    it. If you hear music in it, lower that card's Amount.
      • 
                    
    • Not built yet and No fix yet. A card marked Not built
                    yet has a fix that is not ready in this version. No fix
                    yet means there is no tested fix at all. You can still pick
                    these cards: Shimmer notes your pick on this computer to help test
                    new fixes, but the sound does not change.
      • 
                    
    • Mastering cards. Lack of air and Loudness
                    change settings in Mastering instead of running a fix. Turning one
                    on turns mastering on.
      • 
                
    

                
    
                    The Shimmer card takes a moment the first time. Its
                    spectral de-noise reads the whole song once, the first time the
                    card is on for a song. That takes about 50 seconds for a
                    3-minute song. The preview status line at the bottom shows how
                    far it has got. After that, the preview is quick, even when you
                    change the Amount.
                
    

                
    Every card, and the fix it runs
    
                
    

                
    Not sure which card?
    
                
    
                    Answer a couple of questions about what you hear, and we'll
                    point you at a card.
                
    
                
    

                
    Presets in Remix and Batch
    
                
    
                    The 1.x presets are still in two places: the Artifact cleanup
                    on export menu in Remix, and the Preset menu in Batch.
                    In 2.0 each preset turns on its matching card, at that card's
                    starting Amount. Preset strength no longer changes the
                    sound, and Batch's Auto-detect each file no longer tries
                    presets on each file. Generic turns on no card. With
                    cleanup on, Fixed tones still cuts steady tones when a song has
                    them.
                
    
                
    
                
    
                    Settings from 1.x: if you used 1.x with Remember
                    settings next time on, the preset you saved turns on its card
                    in the Master tab. After that, 2.0 keeps your own card picks.
                
    
            
    

            
    
                
    Advanced artifact controls
    
                
    
                    These are the 1.x cleaning controls. They are still in the
                    Advanced artifact controls drawer, but in 2.0 they do not
                    change the sound, and neither does Preset strength. The
                    Master tab's cleaning now comes from the What do you hear?
                    cards and each card's Amount. The notes below say what
                    each control did in 1.x.
                
    
                
    
            
    

            
    
                
    Troubleshooting
    
                
    
                    Find the problem you can actually hear, then follow the fix.
                    Always compare against your Original (key 1) and
                    listen to the Removed track (key 3). If you hear
                    real music in Removed, a fix is cutting too hard — that is
                    the single most useful check in the whole app.
                
    

                
    I still hear the problem after cleaning
    

                
    
                    
    A fizzy, flickering hiss on the cymbals and vocals
    
                    
    What is happening: this is the fizz AI music tools
                    leave in the top end, often called Suno hash.
    
                    
    Fix: turn on the Shimmer card. It runs the
                    spectral de-noise. If fizz is left, raise its Amount while
                    the Removed track still holds only hiss. This fix is new in 2.0
                    and still being tested; on some songs it takes out less than you
                    want.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    A whistle or whine that never changes pitch
    
                    
    What is happening: the generator left a steady tone at
                    one pitch for the whole song.
    
                    
    Fix: click Analyze. When it finds steady tones,
                    it turns on Fixed tones, and a notch filter cuts each one.
                    Fixed tones only cuts tones that hold one pitch, so a whistle
                    that slides up and down is not caught.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    A grainy hiss on the voice that never quite goes away
    
                    
    Fix: turn on Vocal grain. It runs the voice
                    de-noise, which takes out a steady hiss, a hiss that follows the
                    voice, and sharp little spikes, strongest at 4–8 kHz. It is
                    often worse later in the song.
    
                    
    The first time you turn it on, Shimmer asks you to check: on
                    a song without grain, this fix takes some sparkle away. Listen
                    to the Removed track (key 3). It should hold only hiss and
                    grit.
    
                    
    Works on: Centre of the mix needs nothing extra.
                    Vocal only splits out the vocal first with the Remix
                    splitter, so cymbals in the centre are left alone. The first
                    split takes a minute or more.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Harsh, spitty "s" and "sh" on the vocals
    
                    
    Fix: turn on Sibilance. It runs a de-esser, which
                    turns down harsh "s", "t" and "ch" only while they stick out. If
                    the voice itself sounds glassy or plastic, try this card too.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Piercing upper mids that come and go
    
                    
    Fix: turn on Harshness. It runs a dynamic EQ that
                    finds the narrow band in the upper mids that sticks out most, and
                    turns it down only while it is louder than usual for the
                    song.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    The mix sounds thick, boxy or muddy
    
                    
    Fix: turn on Low-mid build-up. It runs a dynamic
                    EQ on the low mids, so the words come through more clearly.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Clicks, pops or crackle
    
                    
    Fix: turn on Clicks and crackle. It runs the
                    de-click. If the card says Not built yet, the de-click is
                    not ready in this version: picking the card only notes it, and
                    the sound does not change.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Grainy or watery reverb tails
    
                    
    This is Phasiness. If the card says No fix yet,
                    Shimmer has no tested fix for it. Picking the card notes it on
                    this computer to help test new fixes; it does not change the
                    sound.
    
                
    

                
    I cut too much — now something sounds wrong
    

                
    
                    
    The result sounds dull or lifeless
    
                    
    What is happening: a fix is taking too much out of the
                    top end.
    
                    
    Fix: lower the Amount on Vocal grain,
                    Shimmer or Sibilance first. Turn off any card for
                    a problem you cannot hear. If the song was dull to start with, try Lack of
                    air, which sets Tilt to Bright in Mastering.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Vocals sound lispy, or the "s" sounds went missing
    
                    
    What is happening: the "s", "t" and "ch" sounds are
                    short bursts of high end, so a strong de-esser can take them
                    along with the harshness.
    
                    
    Fix: lower the Amount on Sibilance.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    The low end or the voice feels thin
    
                    
    Fix: lower the Amount on Low-mid
                    build-up, or turn it off. Then check the Parametric EQ for a
                    low-cut or a cut you did not mean. Mastering's own low-cut only
                    removes the deep rumble below the bass.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Cymbals lost their sparkle
    
                    
    What is happening: cymbals share the top end with the
                    fizz, so a deep cut can take some of their shine.
    
                    
    Fix: lower the Amount on Shimmer. With
                    Vocal grain on, set its Works on to Vocal
                    only, so it leaves the cymbals alone.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    A warble, or an "underwater" sound, appeared
    
                    
    What is happening: spectral de-noise can leave a watery
                    sound when it cuts deep.
    
                    
    Fix: lower the Amount on Shimmer. Try
                    halving it and listen again.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    The Removed track has real music in it
    
                    
    What is happening: a fix is treating part of your song
                    as noise. This is the most important problem in this list,
                    because you will not always hear the damage on the Processed
                    track — but it is there.
    
                    
    Fix: turn cards off one at a time to find which fix is
                    taking music. Then lower its Amount until Removed holds
                    only hiss, fizz or whistles.
    
                
    

                
    Common questions
    

                
    
                    
    The preview takes a while after I turn on Shimmer
    
                    
    That is expected. The first time the Shimmer card is on
                    for a song, its spectral de-noise reads the whole song once:
                    about 50 seconds for a 3-minute song. The preview status line at
                    the bottom shows how far it has got. After that, the preview is
                    quick.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    I want more cleanup than 100%
    
                    
    Amount stops at 100%, the deepest cut each fix allows.
                    If that is not enough, the card may not match what you hear.
                    Listen to Removed: if it holds the wrong sound, try a different
                    card, or answer the questions on the What do you hear? tab
                    again with fresh ears.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Which card should I start with?
    
                    
    Two ways to get there:
    
                    
      
                        
    1. Click Analyze on the Master tab. Shimmer measures
                        your track's loudness, tone and fixed tones, and marks what it
                        found on the What do you hear? cards.
      2. 
                        
    2. Use the What do you hear? tab in this Help and
                        answer a couple of questions about what you hear.
      3. 
                    
    
                    
    Both are just starting points. Trust your ears over either one.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    My track has two problems at once
    
                    
    That is normal. Turn on a card for each one. Each card runs its
                    own fix, in a set order. The Signal Chain tab shows what
                    each fix does to your song.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    Where did the presets go?
    
                    
    The Master tab uses the What do you hear? cards instead.
                    Remix and Batch still offer the 1.x presets, and each one turns
                    on its matching card. The What do you hear? tab has the
                    full list.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    My export is quieter than other songs
    
                    
    Check that Master for release is on, then look at the
                    Loudness target. Commercial, the default, is as loud
                    as most released songs. Balanced is a little quieter, with
                    more punch left in. Streaming standard is the level
                    streaming apps play songs at, so it sounds quiet in other players.
                    Each choice shows its LUFS level.
    
                    
    With mastering off, Preserve volume keeps the song at
                    its own level, so a cleaning-only file plays as loud as the file
                    you loaded. After export, the Release check shows how loud
                    the file will play on each service.
    
                
    
            
    

            
    
                
    Setup
    
                
    
                    Shimmer works out of the box for most files. Two features need
                    a one-time extra install.
                
    

                
    MP3 and M4A files
    
                
    
                    WAV, FLAC, and OGG work right away. MP3, M4A, and AAC need a
                    free helper called ffmpeg — it is the tool that
                    reads and writes compressed audio.
                
    

                
    
                    
    Windows
    
                    
    Open a terminal and run:
    
                    
    winget install ffmpeg
    
                    
    Then restart Shimmer.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    macOS and Linux
    
                    
    macOS: brew install ffmpeg
    
                    Linux: apt install ffmpeg
    
                    
    Or download from
                        ffmpeg.org
                        and add it to your PATH.
                    
    
                
    

                
    Stem separation (the Remix tab)
    

                
    
                    
    First run downloads the separation engine
    
                    
    Splitting a song into stems needs a large one-time download
                    (several GB for the engine). Shimmer installs it in its own
                    separate folder so it never interferes with the rest of the app.
                    The Best quality tier fetches its own model the first time
                    you pick it (about 330 MB); Fast and 6 stems are
                    about 80 MB each, Ultra adds 160 MB, and Studio
                    installs the RoFormer runner (about 300 MB) plus its 915 MB
                    vocal model.
    
                    
    If you have an NVIDIA graphics card, separation uses it
                    automatically: a four-minute song takes about 10 seconds at Fast
                    and 25 at Best. Without one it still works, just several times
                    slower. Results are saved per track and per quality, so you only
                    ever wait once for a given song. The Remix tab's empty screen
                    says which engine and card it found.
    
                
    
                
    
                    
    The models, who made them, and what you may do with them
    
                    
    Shimmer does not ship any model. Each one downloads the first
                    time you pick its split, from its author's own release, into
                    stem_cache next to the app, and stays there; after
                    that the Remix tab works offline. A model file you place in
                    stem_cache/models yourself (same file name) is used
                    as it is. Every model offered is under a licence that lets you
                    use it on music you release and sell; models whose authors have
                    published no terms are not offered.

    Which stems can it make?
    
                    
    The open separator Shimmer runs (Demucs) splits a mix into
                    vocals, drums, bass and other; the 6-stem tier adds
                    guitar and piano. Synths, keys, strings and effects
                    all land in Other. There is no open model that pulls those apart,
                    and tools that show a synth or strings lane for every song are
                    regenerating parts rather than splitting your mix, which is why
                    their lanes do not add back up to the original.
    
                    
    Shimmer keeps a Residual lane instead: the mix minus
                    every stem, which is whatever the separator dropped (reverb tails,
                    room, most of the AI fizz). With it in the mix, an untouched remix
                    is identical to your original; the Residual figure in the mixer
                    header says how much is in it.

                    once it is installed.
    
                
    

                
    
                    
    How the fixes work
    
                    
    Most of the fixes are classic signal processing, the same
                    kind of math behind an EQ or a compressor: a notch filter, a
                    de-esser and a dynamic EQ. The one exception is the
                    Shimmer card's spectral de-noise. It uses a small trained
                    model (a neural network) that runs on your own computer and
                    turns down only what it hears as fizz. Nothing is sent anywhere,
                    and the same song with the same settings gives the same result
                    each time.
